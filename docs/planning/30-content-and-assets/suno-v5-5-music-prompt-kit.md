# Riverbend Airport — Suno v5.5 Music Prompts

## M01 — Riverbend Morning / Main Menu

> Instrumental piano-led early rock-and-roll with a warm New Orleans rhythm-and-blues bounce, 100 BPM. Open with one instantly hummable four-bar piano riff, then answer it with soft tenor sax and muted trumpet. Upright piano, round electric bass, brushed-to-stick drums, light handclaps only at section turns, clean rhythm guitar tucked low. Optimistic small regional airport at sunrise: capable, welcoming, quietly ambitious. A memorable hook returns three times with small harmonic variations. Spacious middle passage for radio chatter; polished analog recording; clean loop-friendly ending. Instrumental only, no vocals, no spoken word, no crowd sounds, no aggressive guitar, no big-band swing, no cinematic orchestra.

## M02 — First Runway / Construction Planning

> Instrumental piano-driven rock-and-roll R&B, 112 BPM, built around a playful five-note piano riff that feels like laying out a new runway. Tight kick and snare, walking electric bass, clipped rhythm guitar, compact tenor sax answers, occasional trumpet punctuation. Energetic but controlled: measuring, placing, connecting, and watching a small airfield take shape. Use short stop-time breaks before the hook returns, then a restrained bridge with only piano, bass, and light percussion for construction UI and radio. Warm analog band, clear ending for looping. Instrumental only; no vocals, no novelty effects, no hard rock, no orchestral drama.

## M03 — Grass-Airfield Operations / Calm Observation

> Instrumental 1950s piano-rock rhythm section with an original, highly hummable rolling piano motif, 106 BPM. Bright acoustic piano leads; relaxed drums, rounded bass, soft sax replies, and occasional clean guitar comping. Picture a hand-built grass airfield working beautifully: a trainer lands, taxis, fuels, and departs while the player watches the system click into place. Maintain gentle forward motion without urgency. Leave frequent breathing room for aircraft and radio audio. Two subtle lift sections, no huge solo, no vocals, no ambient airport recordings, no blues ballad, no lounge music. Finish with a loop-safe final piano turnaround.

## M04 — Living GA Airport / Busy Productive Day

> Instrumental piano-led R&B rock-and-roll at 120 BPM. A catchy syncopated piano hook drives a cheerful working airport: flight school circuits, charter arrivals, service vehicles, and a growing ramp. Punchy but not harsh drums, elastic bass, short call-and-response sax and trumpet figures, clean guitar rhythm in the background. The motif must be easy to hum after one listen. Alternate lively full-band passages with lean piano-and-bass passages that leave room for operational radio. Confident, tactile, polished analog production. Instrumental only; no vocals, no shouted hooks, no distorted guitars, no frantic drum fills, no jazz improvisation showcase.

## M05 — Passenger Terminal and Landside Flow

> Instrumental piano rock-and-roll with warm rhythm-and-blues swing, 116 BPM. Create an original two-part hook: piano carries the first half, a friendly tenor sax answers the second. Busy but graceful passenger terminal energy: check-in, bags, boarding, curbside, buses, and families moving with purpose. Acoustic piano, bass, compact drums, restrained trumpet accents, and a light guitar pulse. Include one spacious mid-track section so captions and boarding calls can stay clear. The music is polished, optimistic, and management-sim focused rather than theatrical. Instrumental only; no vocals, no crowd noise, no Broadway feel, no big-band brass wall, no EDM or rock breakdown.

## M06 — Regional Timetable / Peak Scheduling

> Instrumental piano-driven early rock-and-roll R&B at 126 BPM, with an irresistible original riff that feels like flights clicking into exact time slots. Fast but composed: crisp drums, steady electric bass, bright piano, short horn stabs, and clean rhythm guitar. The arrangement should suggest a seven-day timetable, multiple operators, gates, buses, rail, and connections without becoming frantic. Build tension through rhythmic variation, not loudness; give the hook a satisfying release every 16 bars. Include a lower-density bridge for schedule changes and radio. Warm analog mix, loop-ready ending. Instrumental only; no vocals, no aggressive soloing, no stadium-rock drums, no cinematic trailer hits.

## M07 — Weather Watch / Reduced Visibility

> Instrumental nocturnal rhythm-and-blues piano cue, 92 BPM. Keep the same warm airport-band identity but slow the groove: minor-tinged piano riff, brushed drums with a firm pulse, round bass, muted tenor sax, and very sparse trumpet. Rain, lower visibility, and careful decisions: alert and focused, never bleak or scary. The central riff must remain hummable and reassuring. Use space and subtle syncopation instead of ominous drones. Make a quiet middle section for weather advisories and captions, then return to a resolved optimistic hook. Instrumental only; no vocals, no thunder samples, no horror, no orchestral suspense, no sad ballad.

## M08 — Incident Response / Controlled Recovery

> Instrumental piano-led R&B rock-and-roll response cue, 112 BPM. Begin with a clipped, focused piano motif and tight drums; add bass, muted trumpet, and tenor sax in short disciplined phrases. This is a safe, abstract airport response: protect the area, keep unaffected operations moving, dispatch resources, diagnose, and recover. Pressure without panic; confidence without triumphalism. A clear hummable motif should gradually open into warmer chords as the situation stabilizes. Leave explicit gaps for urgent captioned radio. Instrumental only; no sirens, no disaster sound design, no voices, no grim orchestral score, no aggressive rock.

## M09 — Milestone / Airport Opens Successfully

It should feel like a runway opening, a successful first flight, or a new airport capability: earned satisfaction, not a victory fanfare. Start quickly, peak once, then return to a clean concise hook for a loop or achievement edit. Warm analog studio sound, small band, highly hummable. Instrumental only; no vocals, no chanting, no orchestral fanfare, no stadium rock, no novelty comedy.
