# Riverbend Airport — Suno v5.5 Retro Flight Music Prompts

**Status:** Draft prompt bank  
**Owner:** Audio and speech  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-27

## Purpose and scope

This optional instrumental prompt bank adds a dynamic retro-flight branch to
the existing piano-led and rockabilly explorations. Its musical direction is
informed by the officially described *SimplePlanes 2* soundtrack: driving
1980s-rock energy, nostalgic retro-electronic warmth, and layered intensity
that can move naturally between calm designing and active flight.

The prompts are for original Riverbend Airport music only. They do not request
named performers, source tracks, recognizable melodies, lyrics, spoken word,
real-operator branding, or unreviewed samples. Retention, provenance,
captions-first mixing, and shipping-rights requirements remain governed by
[CT-03](radio-audio-speech.md).

## Base set

## M29 — Designer Skyline / Main Menu

> Instrumental retro-electronic and 1980s-rock main-menu cue, 96 BPM. Open with an original luminous polysynth figure over a warm analog pad, then introduce a gentle electronic pulse, rounded synth bass, clean chorus electric-guitar answers, and compact gated drums. It should feel like viewing a quiet regional airport from above before the day begins: inventive, welcoming, and ready to build. Keep the melody instantly memorable but leave broad open space for menu navigation and captions. Use a calm designer section with lighter drums, then a modest flight-ready lift, and end on a clean loop-friendly resolution. Instrumental only.

## M30 — Vector Draft / Construction Planning

> Instrumental synth-rock planning cue, 110 BPM. Build an original rhythmic arpeggiator hook with warm analog bass, tight electronic kick and snare, bright clean electric guitar, compact tom fills, and small sparkling synthesizer accents. The music should suggest laying out a runway, connecting roads, placing a warehouse, and watching a clear plan become an airport. Maintain measured forward motion and crisp transitions for construction UI. Include a lean middle passage with arpeggio, bass, and light percussion for radio and captions, then return to a confident repeatable hook with a clean loop seam. Instrumental only.

## M31 — First Circuit / Grass-Airfield Operations

> Instrumental retro-flight electronic rock cue, 116 BPM. An original soaring synth lead carries a short, hummable four-bar theme over steady synth bass, punchy but restrained gated drums, clean electric-guitar rhythm, and airy pad chords. Small aircraft take off, taxi, refuel, and return to a hand-built grass airfield; the feeling is capable, bright, and quietly exhilarating. Shape the arrangement in layers: a calmer observing passage, a fuller active-flight passage, then a relaxed return that preserves operational clarity. Leave generous breathing room for aircraft sound and radio. Finish with a loop-ready melodic turnaround. Instrumental only.

## M32 — Copper Altitude / Living GA and Flight School

> Instrumental upbeat 1980s electronic rock, 122 BPM. Lead with an original syncopated synthesizer riff and clean chime-like guitar response, supported by warm bass synth, crisp drum-machine groove, restrained live tom accents, and bright sustained pad harmony. Flight-school circuits, charter arrivals, ground teams, and service vehicles should feel busy and coordinated rather than frantic. Build one optimistic lift for a successful movement, then reduce to bass, pulse, and a light lead phrase for operational radio. Give the hook a satisfying return every sixteen bars and use a polished loop-friendly ending. Instrumental only.

## M33 — Runway Network / Timetable and Mixed Operations

> Instrumental driving retro-electronic airport-management cue, 126 BPM. Create an original precise sequenced-synth motif that interlocks with disciplined drums, elastic analog bass, clean electric-guitar chords, short brass-like synth punctuations, and a wide but controlled pad. The rhythm should evoke recurring flights, cargo handoffs, gates, buses, rail links, and shared resources resolving on time. Build energy through layered counter-rhythms and clear harmonic release rather than louder dynamics. Include a low-density planning bridge with pulse and bass for timetable adjustments and captions, then resolve into a compact repeatable hook. Instrumental only.

## M34 — Crosswind Protocol / Weather and Controlled Recovery

> Instrumental nocturnal synth-rock operations cue, 94 BPM. Begin with an original minor-color synthesizer phrase, steady electronic pulse, rounded analog bass, soft gated snare, distant clean-guitar harmonics, and a warm sustained pad. Reduced visibility and careful recovery should feel alert, technical, and reassuring; retain a clear forward motion and a memorable central melody. Let the arrangement open gradually as safe operations resume, using a sparse advisory section for weather calls and captions before a confident resolved return. Polished retro electronic production with a clean loop-friendly ending. Instrumental only.

## M35 — Starlit Ramp / Late-Night Planning

> Instrumental after-hours retro-electronic airport cue, 82 BPM. A gentle original analog-synth motif moves over soft pulse percussion, low warm bass, glassy electric-guitar swells, and a broad nocturnal pad. Quiet ramp lights, late cargo receipts, maintenance checks, and thoughtful planning should feel focused, hopeful, and unhurried. Keep the melody simple enough to remember after one listen and the arrangement spacious for menus, captions, and radio. Add one subtle electric-guitar lift, then return to the intimate synth figure for a seamless loop point. Instrumental only.

## M36 — Open Horizon / Capability Milestone

> Instrumental celebratory 1980s synth-rock cue, 130 BPM. Start immediately with an original eight-bar synth-and-guitar hook, buoyant analog bass, punchy gated drums, bright pad chords, and a concise lead-guitar answer. It should feel like opening a new runway, completing a cargo surge, proving an Advanced capability, or seeing a thriving airport work as one system: earned progress, open skies, and forward momentum. Reach one clean peak, leave a short open passage for an achievement caption, and return to a concise uplifting hook that supports a loop or result edit. Instrumental only.

## Retention gate

Before retaining any generated audio, record the generator/service, exact
prompt, request identity or seed, duration, output checksum, post-processing,
intended mix-group use, and approval or rejection. Preserve source masters
outside cooked content and import only an approved derived asset.
